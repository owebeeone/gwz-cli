## 1. Findings

### [A-P2-1] NEW ARCHITECTURAL — the family lock does not wait and cannot be held across the clone, so the mechanism that cures N-P2-1 is not realisable against gwz-core

**Location:** object lines 112-113 (section 1), 269-273 (D5), 296-303 (D6), 469-473 (S1.1), 459-460 (S1.0); `gwz-core/crates/family-store-contract/src/lib.rs:284-288`, `gwz-core/crates/family-store/src/lib.rs:248-266`, `gwz-core/src/filesystem/native/platform_lock.rs:7-25`, `gwz-core/src/local_clone/create.rs:14, 184, 216-218`.

**Root cause:** the round-2 patch resolved a cross-process atomicity problem by asserting a lock discipline — a contending caller waits, and one holder spans the whole clone — that gwz-core's family lock does not provide and that this plan does not own the code to add.

**Violated invariant:** a decision may rest on a capability of a cited system only if that system has it. Section 1 is the plan's "facts this plan rests on"; the serialization sentence there is an inference the plan drew, not a quotation, and it is contradicted by the cited system's own contract.

**Reproduction / state sequence.** Three manifestations of one root cause.

*(i) The second handler does not wait; it fails.* D5 lines 269-272: "the second handler, **which waits on that lock**, sees the `ready` row and the record together, or neither; it then reuses ... and prints the same path, so Claude receives one path twice." The contract says otherwise: `try_lock` is documented "Take the root family lock or refuse `Busy`" (contract:284-288), the implementation returns `StoreError::Busy` (family-store:264), and the syscall is `flock(..., LOCK_EX | LOCK_NB)` (platform_lock:12). So with two differing handlers — the configuration D5 now permits and `setup` only warns about — handler B calls in while A holds the lock for the duration of a multi-minute clone (S1.0 sizes it), receives `Busy`, and under D6 line 309-310 ("Every other combination refuses: exit non-zero") exits non-zero. Section 1 line 45: "Any non-zero exit aborts creation." Claude aborts the creation A completed, and A's lane is orphaned. This is precisely the outcome my round-2 N-P2-1 described, reinstated through the mechanism intended to prevent it. D5 lines 274-281 do state an orphan case, but scope it to "a reason of its own (a pinned `--command` naming a binary without the `hook` family, or a malformed handler)" — lock contention, the common case, is not among them, because D5 believes it cannot happen.

*(ii) The hook cannot hold the lock across the clone.* D6 lines 299-302: "The hook takes the family lock **before it reads the index** and holds it until the clone has committed the `ready` row and the hook has written its own session record". S1.1 lines 469-473 spells the same sequence out: "takes the family lock, evaluates the reuse table first ... runs the local clone in process ..., writes the sidecar record, releases the lock". But `create.rs` takes the lock itself at step 5 (`:216-218`), after a step-4 inventory the source comments place explicitly "before the family lock" (`:14`, `:184`), and the `FamilyStore` contract offers no caller-held-lock variant — `try_lock` returns the sole `Session` handle and `create` constructs its own. A hook that holds the lock and then runs the clone in process meets its own `flock`, which on both supported POSIX platforms is per-open-file-description and returns `WouldBlock` for a second acquisition from the same process. The clone fails busy. The stated lock scope is therefore not implementable as written, which means the row-and-record atomicity the whole table depends on has no mechanism.

*(iii) Section 1's serialization fact is an unsupported inference.* Line 112-113: "Family commands serialize on that lock, so two creations at once run one after the other rather than failing." The quoted source, `LocalClones.md:145`, says only that the lock "serializes family commands" — mutual exclusion, which is true. The clause "rather than failing" is the plan's own addition and is false against contract:284-288. This sentence has been in the document since before round 1 (I verified the quotation in round 1 but not the inference drawn from it, which I record as a miss of mine); it became load-bearing only when this patch built the atomicity cure on top of it, and it now also underwrites S1.0's timeout guidance at lines 459-460 ("sized for two copies back to back, since two creations serialize on the family lock").

**Impact:** the central claim of the C-N-P2-1 remediation is unrealisable, so N-P2-1's failure mode returns in the ordinary contention case rather than only in the interrupted one — a creation reported as failed to the user while the 65 GB artefact was in fact created and left behind, retirable only through S3.4's L1 waiver procedure. D6's eight-row table, which is otherwise exhaustive and well-formed, has no row for `Busy` because the document believes `Busy` cannot occur. U4's resolution (lines 168-170) is explicitly conditional on the D6 lock sentence and therefore falls with it. S1.1's tests would be written against a fixture in which the assumed semantics are simply assumed, so the gap would not surface until an implementer met `StoreError::Busy` — at which point the fix is no longer a document edit.

**Required correction — and why it is not a text fix.** The document cannot be made true by rewording, because every available repair changes the mechanism or its owner:
1. *Give gwz-core the primitive.* A waiting (or bounded-retry) family lock, and a `local clone` entry point that accepts a caller-held session. That is gwz-core work with its own requirements, design and review — it belongs in `GwzLaneCleanFixes.md` or a sibling document, and D7 states that this plan consumes gwz-core requirements rather than authoring them. It also adds a second out-of-plan release dependency alongside the one at sketch line 726.
2. *Tolerate `Busy` in the hook.* Add a bounded retry with a stated budget against Claude's timeout, add the `Busy` row to D6's table, and re-scope D5's orphan paragraph to include contention. This keeps the work in gwz-cli but changes the concurrency design and needs its own test set and its own review of whether a retry can deterministically finish within S1.0's timeout.
3. *Abandon cross-handler reconciliation.* Return to refusing a second placement (the pre-round-1 D5), or make a `Busy` second handler a *success* that reads the `ready` row and prints its path without the lock — which reintroduces a read-without-lock and must be reasoned about afresh.

Whichever route the operator takes, section 1 lines 112-113 must be corrected to the contract's actual semantics first, since three other statements rest on it.

**Closure test:** every sentence in the object that asserts a lock behaviour cites the gwz-core contract that provides it (`FamilyStore::try_lock`, or a named successor), and D6's table has a row for a busy lock with a stated outcome. ## 3. Risks and next action

Across three rounds this document has improved a great deal, and the round-2 patch in particular did real work: D6's outcome table is exhaustive and fail-closed, the guards-after-reuse rule is right, the ignore check now covers everything the hooks write, and the asymmetries that were previously silent are now stated with reasons. All twelve findings I raised in rounds 1 and 2 are closed, and I re-verified each against my own original counterexample rather than against the drafter's claim.

The reason this is nonetheless a NO-GO, and why I offer no pre-commit, is that the fix for N-P2-1 reached outside what a plan document can settle. To make a two-process reconciliation safe, the patch needed a lock that waits and a lock that spans the clone; it asserted both, and gwz-core provides neither — `try_lock` refuses `Busy` at the contract, at the implementation and at the `flock(..., LOCK_NB)` syscall, and `local clone` takes the lock itself after an explicitly pre-lock inventory. That is not a sentence to repair. It is a choice among three routes (a new gwz-core primitive; a bounded-retry design inside gwz-cli; or abandoning cross-handler reconciliation), and each of them is a design decision with its own review, not a remediation edit. Offering a pre-commit here would misrepresent the size of the work, and the loop's remediation cap is exhausted in any case.

I would add one observation for whoever picks this up, without pressing it as a finding: the shortest honest route may be the third. The original D5 forbade two placements; round 1 removed the prohibition because its stated reason had evaporated under D1's fixed handler text — which was correct — but the replacement took on a concurrency problem to support a configuration that D5 itself calls rare (line 282-284) and that S4.1 recommends against. A design that refuses or warns-and-accepts-the-abort, and simply documents the orphan, needs no lock semantics at all.

Recommended next action: the lane owner escalates to the operator with A-P2-1 and the three routes, rather than attempting a third remediation round. P3-1 (one clause in D8) and P3-2 (two clauses in S4.1) are genuine but small, and should ride along with whatever revision the chosen route produces rather than justify a round of their own. Section 1 lines 112-113 should be corrected to the contract's actual semantics in any case, since three other statements in the document rest on it.
